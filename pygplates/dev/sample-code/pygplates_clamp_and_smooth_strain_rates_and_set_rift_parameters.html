Clamp and smooth strain rates, and set rift parameters

This example changes how the strain rates of deforming networks are calculated, using pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters and the rift parameters of a network feature.

The strain rate at a point in a deforming network comes from the triangulation of the network, in which each triangle has one strain rate (see Query the triangulation and rigid blocks of deforming networks). Three things about it can be changed:

  • Clamping limits the strain rate of every triangle to a maximum. Depending on how a network was built, some of its triangles can have excessive strain rates.

  • Smoothing is how the strain rate at a point is interpolated from the triangles around it.

  • Rift parameters say that a network is one side of a rift between two plates. The strain rate then increases exponentially across the network, from the un-stretched side of the rift to the rift axis, where otherwise it would be much the same all the way across.

All three change whatever is calculated from strain rates as well, such as the accumulated strain, crustal thickness and tectonic subsidence of points reconstructed through the networks (see Reconstruct strain and strain rate and Reconstruct crustal thickness and tectonic subsidence).

The first example compares strain rates with and without clamping, and with each kind of smoothing. The second gives the networks of a rift their rift parameters, and compares the strain rates across the rift.

Clamp and smooth strain rates

Data files

topologies.gpml

Topological features that include deforming networks. The parameters in this example only affect deforming networks. There must be networks at 10Ma, since the points are chosen from them. Leave out inactive networks, such as those in Zahirovic_etal_2022_Deforming_Networks_Inactive.gpml in the GPlates sample data, so that the points are chosen from networks that are deforming.

rotations.rot

A rotation file. It must contain rotations for the plate IDs of the features that the deforming networks are built from.

Files of these kinds are in the GPlates sample data.

Sample code

import sys
import pygplates


# Load the topological features and the rotations once, since several topological models are created from them.
topological_features = pygplates.FeatureCollection('topologies.gpml')
rotation_model = pygplates.RotationModel('rotations.rot')

# The strain rates are compared at 10Ma, at points in the deforming networks.
# The points are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart,
# from latitude 60 to -60 (north to south).
time = 10
grid_points = [(latitude, longitude) for latitude in range(60, -70, -10) for longitude in range(-180, 180, 10)]

# Find where each grid point is in the topologies resolved at 10Ma. Keep the points in the deforming region of a
# network, and take the first four of them, so that the output stays short.
grid_point_locations = pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot(
        topological_features, rotation_model, time).get_point_locations(grid_points)
points = [point for point, point_location in zip(grid_points, grid_point_locations)
          if point_location.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region()][:4]

# Stop if there are none: there are no deforming networks at 10Ma, or they are too small for the grid.
if not points:
    sys.exit(f'No grid point is in the deforming region of a network at {time}Ma')

# The default parameters for resolving topologies:
# strain rates are not clamped, and they are smoothed using natural neighbour interpolation.
default_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters()

# Clamp the total strain rate of each triangle (of a deforming network) to a maximum of 5e-16 per second.
clamped_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        enable_strain_rate_clamping=True,
        max_clamped_strain_rate=5e-16)

# Do not smooth strain rates. Each point then has the strain rate of the triangle that contains it.
unsmoothed_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        strain_rate_smoothing=pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.none)

# Smooth strain rates using barycentric interpolation (instead of natural neighbour interpolation).
barycentric_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        strain_rate_smoothing=pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.barycentric)


def print_strain_rates(name, topological_model):
    """Print the strain rates that a topological model gives at the points."""

    # Get a snapshot of the topologies resolved at 10Ma, and the strain rate at each point.
    topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)
    point_strain_rates = topological_snapshot.get_point_strain_rates(points)

    # The largest total strain rate of all the triangles of all the deforming networks.
    largest_triangle_strain_rate = max(
            (triangle.strain_rate.get_total_strain_rate()
             for resolved_network in topological_snapshot.get_resolved_topologies(pygplates.ResolveTopologyType.network)
             for triangle in resolved_network.get_network_triangulation().get_triangles()),
            default=0.0)

    print(f'{name}:')
    print(f'  largest total strain rate of a triangle: {largest_triangle_strain_rate:.3e} 1/s')
    for point, point_strain_rate in zip(points, point_strain_rates):
        # A point that is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) has no strain rate.
        if point_strain_rate is not None:
            print(f'  total strain rate at {point}: {point_strain_rate.get_total_strain_rate():.3e} 1/s')


for name, parameters in [
        ('default', default_parameters),
        ('clamped', clamped_parameters),
        ('not smoothed', unsmoothed_parameters),
        ('barycentric smoothing', barycentric_parameters)]:

    # Create a topological model that resolves its deforming networks using the parameters.
    topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel(
            topological_features,
            rotation_model,
            default_resolve_topology_parameters=parameters)

    print_strain_rates(name, topological_model)

# Parameters can also be given to one feature collection (or file), by pairing it with them in a tuple.
# They are then used for the deforming networks in that feature collection, in place of the default parameters.
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel(
        [(topological_features, clamped_parameters)],
        rotation_model)

print_strain_rates('clamped, with the parameters given to the feature collection', topological_model)

Details

The topological features are loaded into a pygplates.FeatureCollection, and the rotations into a pygplates.RotationModel.

A topological model can be created from file names instead. But this example creates several topological models, and this way the files are only read once.

# Load the topological features and the rotations once, since several topological models are created from them.
topological_features = pygplates.FeatureCollection('topologies.gpml')
rotation_model = pygplates.RotationModel('rotations.rot')

The strain rates are compared at 10Ma, at four points in deforming networks. The points are chosen from the networks, rather than written into the script, so that the example works with any topological features that have deforming networks at that time.

They are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart, from latitude 60 to -60. It stops at 60 degrees because, in the GPlates sample data, the grid points nearer the poles happen to fall where the networks barely deform at 10Ma, so they would make poor examples. The grid is scanned from north to south, and the first points found are used.

# The strain rates are compared at 10Ma, at points in the deforming networks.
# The points are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart,
# from latitude 60 to -60 (north to south).
time = 10
grid_points = [(latitude, longitude) for latitude in range(60, -70, -10) for longitude in range(-180, 180, 10)]

A pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot resolves the topologies at one time, here 10Ma, and pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.get_point_locations() returns the location of each grid point in them.

pygplates.TopologyPointLocation.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region() returns the network whose deforming region contains a point. It returns None for a point anywhere else: in a rigid plate, in a rigid block of a network, or outside all the topologies. The first four points in a deforming region are kept.

The snapshot resolves the topologies with the default parameters. That does not change which points are found: the parameters below change the strain rates in a network, not where its deforming region is.

If no grid point is in a deforming region, there is nothing to compare, so the script stops with an error message that says so.

# Find where each grid point is in the topologies resolved at 10Ma. Keep the points in the deforming region of a
# network, and take the first four of them, so that the output stays short.
grid_point_locations = pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot(
        topological_features, rotation_model, time).get_point_locations(grid_points)
points = [point for point, point_location in zip(grid_points, grid_point_locations)
          if point_location.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region()][:4]

# Stop if there are none: there are no deforming networks at 10Ma, or they are too small for the grid.
if not points:
    sys.exit(f'No grid point is in the deforming region of a network at {time}Ma')

A pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters holds the parameters that topologies are resolved with.

Created without arguments, it has the default value of every parameter: strain rates are not clamped, and they are smoothed using natural neighbour interpolation.

# The default parameters for resolving topologies:
# strain rates are not clamped, and they are smoothed using natural neighbour interpolation.
default_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters()

Each of the other sets of parameters changes one thing, and leaves the rest at their defaults.

Clamping is turned on by enable_strain_rate_clamping, and max_clamped_strain_rate is the maximum (in units of 1/second).

It is the total strain rate of a triangle that is limited to the maximum. That is the magnitude of the strain rate, including both its normal and shear components. When it is reduced, every component of the strain rate is reduced in proportion, so the dilatation rate goes down by the same factor.

The default maximum is 5e-15 per second. This example uses a smaller one.

# Clamp the total strain rate of each triangle (of a deforming network) to a maximum of 5e-16 per second.
clamped_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        enable_strain_rate_clamping=True,
        max_clamped_strain_rate=5e-16)

The strain_rate_smoothing argument takes one of the values of pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.

With pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.none the strain rate at a point is that of the triangle that contains the point. So it is the same everywhere in a triangle, and it jumps at the edge between two triangles.

With pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.barycentric it is interpolated linearly from the strain rates at the three vertices of that triangle.

With pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.natural_neighbour, which is the default, it is interpolated from the strain rates at the vertices near the point, using natural neighbour interpolation.

(The strain rate at a vertex is an average of the strain rates of the deforming triangles around it.)

# Do not smooth strain rates. Each point then has the strain rate of the triangle that contains it.
unsmoothed_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        strain_rate_smoothing=pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.none)

# Smooth strain rates using barycentric interpolation (instead of natural neighbour interpolation).
barycentric_parameters = pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
        strain_rate_smoothing=pygplates.StrainRateSmoothing.barycentric)

The function print_strain_rates() prints the strain rates that one topological model gives.

def print_strain_rates(name, topological_model):
    """Print the strain rates that a topological model gives at the points."""

The topologies are resolved at 10Ma, and pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.get_point_strain_rates() returns the strain rate at each point. It is these that smoothing changes.

# Get a snapshot of the topologies resolved at 10Ma, and the strain rate at each point.
topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)
point_strain_rates = topological_snapshot.get_point_strain_rates(points)

The function also finds the largest total strain rate of any triangle of any deforming network, to show what clamping does. With clamping it is the maximum that was asked for (unless no triangle reached it).

Clamping is applied to the triangles. The strain rate at a point is then interpolated from the clamped triangles, so it is no more than the maximum either.

The triangles of a network come from its triangulation. A triangle that is not in the deforming region of its network has a strain rate of zero, so it makes no difference here.

# The largest total strain rate of all the triangles of all the deforming networks.
largest_triangle_strain_rate = max(
        (triangle.strain_rate.get_total_strain_rate()
         for resolved_network in topological_snapshot.get_resolved_topologies(pygplates.ResolveTopologyType.network)
         for triangle in resolved_network.get_network_triangulation().get_triangles()),
        default=0.0)

The largest strain rate of a triangle is printed, followed by the total strain rate at each point.

print(f'{name}:')
print(f'  largest total strain rate of a triangle: {largest_triangle_strain_rate:.3e} 1/s')
for point, point_strain_rate in zip(points, point_strain_rates):
    # A point that is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) has no strain rate.
    if point_strain_rate is not None:
        print(f'  total strain rate at {point}: {point_strain_rate.get_total_strain_rate():.3e} 1/s')

Each set of parameters is visited with a name to print.

for name, parameters in [
        ('default', default_parameters),
        ('clamped', clamped_parameters),
        ('not smoothed', unsmoothed_parameters),
        ('barycentric smoothing', barycentric_parameters)]:

A topological model resolves its topologies using the parameters it is given as its default_resolve_topology_parameters argument. Without that argument it uses the default parameters.

The parameters cannot be changed afterwards, so a topological model is created for each set of parameters.

# Create a topological model that resolves its deforming networks using the parameters.
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel(
        topological_features,
        rotation_model,
        default_resolve_topology_parameters=parameters)

Parameters can also be given to part of a topological model.

A topological model can be created from a list of feature collections, and each of them can be paired with its own parameters, in a 2-tuple. Its deforming networks are then resolved using those parameters, and the networks in the others using the default_resolve_topology_parameters.

The same goes for a file name, a feature or a sequence of features in place of a feature collection.

Here there is only the one feature collection. The model gives the same strain rates as the one created with the same parameters above.

# Parameters can also be given to one feature collection (or file), by pairing it with them in a tuple.
# They are then used for the deforming networks in that feature collection, in place of the default parameters.
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel(
        [(topological_features, clamped_parameters)],
        rotation_model)

print_strain_rates('clamped, with the parameters given to the feature collection', topological_model)

Give a rift an exponential stretching profile

This example takes the deforming networks of a rift, makes copies of them that have rift parameters, and compares the strain rates across the rift with and without the parameters.

Data files

topologies.gpml

The deforming network features of a rift, and the features that their boundaries are built from. A rift is usually modelled with two networks, one on each side of the rift axis. Each lies between an un-stretched margin, which moves with one of the two plates, and the rift axis. In this example every network in the file is taken to be part of the one rift, between South America (plate 201) and Africa (plate 701).

rotations.rot

A rotation file. It must contain rotations for the two plates of the rift, and for the plate IDs of the features that the deforming networks are built from.

Sample code

import pygplates


# The plate IDs of the two plates that the rift is between (here South America and Africa).
rift_left_plate_id = 201
rift_right_plate_id = 701

# Load the topological features: the deforming networks of the rift, and the features their boundaries are built from.
topological_features = pygplates.FeatureCollection('topologies.gpml')
# Load the rotations.
rotation_model = pygplates.RotationModel('rotations.rot')

# Make a second list of the features, in which each deforming network is replaced by a copy that has rift parameters.
rift_topological_features = []
for feature in topological_features:

    # A feature that is not a deforming network goes into the second list as it is.
    if feature.get_feature_type() != pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_topological_network:
        rift_topological_features.append(feature)
        continue

    # Create a network feature with the same topological geometry, name and valid time as the deforming network,
    # and with the plate IDs of the rift.
    rift_network_feature = pygplates.Feature.create_topological_network_feature(
            feature.get_topological_geometry(),
            name=feature.get_name(),
            valid_time=feature.get_valid_time(),
            rift_parameters=(rift_left_plate_id, rift_right_plate_id))
    rift_network_feature.set_reconstruction_plate_id(feature.get_reconstruction_plate_id())

    rift_topological_features.append(rift_network_feature)

# Three topological models of the rift, each with a name to print.
topological_models = {

    # The networks as they were loaded, without rift parameters.
    'without rift parameters': pygplates.TopologicalModel(topological_features, rotation_model),

    # The networks that have rift parameters.
    'with rift parameters': pygplates.TopologicalModel(rift_topological_features, rotation_model),

    # The same, but with an exponential stretching constant of 2 (the default is 1).
    'with rift parameters and a stretching constant of 2': pygplates.TopologicalModel(
            rift_topological_features,
            rotation_model,
            default_resolve_topology_parameters=pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
                    rift_exponential_stretching_constant=2.0)),
}

# Points along a profile across one side of the rift at 122Ma, as (latitude, longitude).
# The profile runs from the un-stretched margin of South America (at longitude 3W) to the rift axis (at longitude 0).
time = 122
profile_points = [(-25, -2.75), (-25, -2.25), (-25, -1.75), (-25, -1.25), (-25, -0.75), (-25, -0.25)]

for name, topological_model in topological_models.items():

    # Get a snapshot of the topologies resolved at 122Ma.
    topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

    # The total number of triangles in the triangulations of the deforming networks.
    num_triangles = 0
    for resolved_network in topological_snapshot.get_resolved_topologies(pygplates.ResolveTopologyType.network):
        num_triangles += len(resolved_network.get_network_triangulation().get_triangles())

    # The strain rate at each point of the profile.
    profile_strain_rates = topological_snapshot.get_point_strain_rates(profile_points)

    print(f'{name}: the networks have {num_triangles} triangles')
    for (latitude, longitude), strain_rate in zip(profile_points, profile_strain_rates):
        # A point that is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) has no strain rate.
        if strain_rate is not None:
            print(f'  dilatation rate at ({latitude}, {longitude}): {strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s')

Details

A rift is between two plates, and its rift parameters start with their two plate IDs. They have to be the plate IDs that the un-stretched margins of the rift move with. A side of a network counts as un-stretched where it moves exactly as one of the two plates does, and the motion of one plate relative to the other gives the direction of stretching.

# The plate IDs of the two plates that the rift is between (here South America and Africa).
rift_left_plate_id = 201
rift_right_plate_id = 701

The features are loaded into a pygplates.FeatureCollection, rather than given to a topological model as a file name, so that the network features can be replaced. The rotations are loaded into a pygplates.RotationModel.

# Load the topological features: the deforming networks of the rift, and the features their boundaries are built from.
topological_features = pygplates.FeatureCollection('topologies.gpml')
# Load the rotations.
rotation_model = pygplates.RotationModel('rotations.rot')

A second list of the features is made, in which each network feature is replaced by a copy that has rift parameters.

# Make a second list of the features, in which each deforming network is replaced by a copy that has rift parameters.
rift_topological_features = []
for feature in topological_features:

Every other feature goes into the second list as it is, so both lists hold the same features for the boundaries of the networks.

# A feature that is not a deforming network goes into the second list as it is.
if feature.get_feature_type() != pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_topological_network:
    rift_topological_features.append(feature)
    continue

pygplates.Feature.create_topological_network_feature() creates a network feature. Its rift_parameters argument is a tuple that begins with the two plate IDs of the rift. It doesn’t matter which is left or right: you can swap them.

They are stored in the feature as the properties gpml:riftLeftPlate and gpml:riftRightPlate. A network that has those two properties is resolved as a rift, and a network without them is not.

The copy is given the topological geometry of the network it replaces (pygplates.Feature.get_topological_geometry() returns it, as a pygplates.GpmlTopologicalNetwork), and its name, valid time and reconstruction plate ID. Any other properties of the original would need to be copied too.

The copy still refers to the features of its boundary, since a topological geometry refers to those features by their feature IDs.

# Create a network feature with the same topological geometry, name and valid time as the deforming network,
# and with the plate IDs of the rift.
rift_network_feature = pygplates.Feature.create_topological_network_feature(
        feature.get_topological_geometry(),
        name=feature.get_name(),
        valid_time=feature.get_valid_time(),
        rift_parameters=(rift_left_plate_id, rift_right_plate_id))
rift_network_feature.set_reconstruction_plate_id(feature.get_reconstruction_plate_id())

rift_topological_features.append(rift_network_feature)

Note

The tuple can have up to three more values after the two plate IDs: the exponential stretching constant, the strain rate resolution and the edge length threshold (in degrees) of that network. Any of them can be None. For example rift_parameters=(201, 701, 2.0) sets the first. A value that is not given comes from the pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters that the network is resolved with, as below.

Three topological models are created: one from the features as they were loaded, and two from the list with the rift networks.

The third is also given a pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters with a rift_exponential_stretching_constant of 2, where the default is 1. The larger the constant, the more the stretching is concentrated towards the rift axis.

The rift parameters of a pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters apply to every rift network that does not have its own value in its feature. They do nothing to a network that has no rift plate IDs.

# Three topological models of the rift, each with a name to print.
topological_models = {

    # The networks as they were loaded, without rift parameters.
    'without rift parameters': pygplates.TopologicalModel(topological_features, rotation_model),

    # The networks that have rift parameters.
    'with rift parameters': pygplates.TopologicalModel(rift_topological_features, rotation_model),

    # The same, but with an exponential stretching constant of 2 (the default is 1).
    'with rift parameters and a stretching constant of 2': pygplates.TopologicalModel(
            rift_topological_features,
            rotation_model,
            default_resolve_topology_parameters=pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters(
                    rift_exponential_stretching_constant=2.0)),
}

The strain rates are compared along a profile across one side of the rift at 122Ma.

The points run from just inside the un-stretched margin of South America to just short of the rift axis. They are positions at 122Ma, and belong to the rift in this example’s data.

# Points along a profile across one side of the rift at 122Ma, as (latitude, longitude).
# The profile runs from the un-stretched margin of South America (at longitude 3W) to the rift axis (at longitude 0).
time = 122
profile_points = [(-25, -2.75), (-25, -2.25), (-25, -1.75), (-25, -1.25), (-25, -0.75), (-25, -0.25)]

For each topological model, the triangles of the resolved networks are counted.

The triangulation of a rift network is refined: more vertices are added to it between the un-stretched side and the rift axis, and they are given velocities that make the strain rate vary exponentially along the direction of stretching. So the rift networks have more triangles.

# The total number of triangles in the triangulations of the deforming networks.
num_triangles = 0
for resolved_network in topological_snapshot.get_resolved_topologies(pygplates.ResolveTopologyType.network):
    num_triangles += len(resolved_network.get_network_triangulation().get_triangles())

pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.get_point_strain_rates() returns the strain rate at each point of the profile.

# The strain rate at each point of the profile.
profile_strain_rates = topological_snapshot.get_point_strain_rates(profile_points)

Finally the dilatation rate at each point of the profile is printed.

Without rift parameters it is much the same along the whole profile. With them it increases from the un-stretched margin to the rift axis, and with the larger stretching constant it increases more steeply.

print(f'{name}: the networks have {num_triangles} triangles')
for (latitude, longitude), strain_rate in zip(profile_points, profile_strain_rates):
    # A point that is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) has no strain rate.
    if strain_rate is not None:
        print(f'  dilatation rate at ({latitude}, {longitude}): {strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s')

Note

How closely the strain rates follow an exponential curve depends on how far the triangulation is refined. That is controlled by the other two rift parameters, pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters.rift_strain_rate_resolution and pygplates.ResolveTopologyParameters.rift_edge_length_threshold_degrees. Smaller values give more triangles and take longer. See Exponential rift stretching profile for the curve.
