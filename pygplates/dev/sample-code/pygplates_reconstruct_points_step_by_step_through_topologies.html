Reconstruct points step by step through topologies

This example reconstructs points through topological plate polygons and deforming networks one time step at a time, from a past geological time to present day.

At each time the topologies are resolved, and pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.reconstruct_points() (added in pyGPlates 1.1) moves each point to where it is one time step later. A point in a rigid plate is rotated with the plate, and a point in a deforming network is deformed with the network.

The script holds the points between one step and the next, so it can do what it likes with them there.

The first example reconstructs all the points together at each step. The second reconstructs each point using the plate or network that contains it, and reports when a point passes from one plate or network to another.

Step by step, or over a time span

pygplates.TopologicalModel.reconstruct_geometry() reconstructs points through the same topologies in the same way, but does all the time steps in one call. It is what Reconstruct strain and strain rate and Reconstruct crustal thickness and tectonic subsidence use.

  • Use pygplates.TopologicalModel.reconstruct_geometry() when all the points are known at the start and their history is wanted. What it returns gives the position of every point at every time step, and its velocity, strain rate, accumulated strain and scalar values (such as crustal thickness and tectonic subsidence). It also deactivates points, such as those on oceanic crust that are subducted.

  • Reconstruct step by step, as on this page, when the script has to act between the steps. For example, to add points along the way (such as new crust at a mid-ocean ridge), to look at which plate each point is in before moving it, or to keep only the current positions of the points rather than their history.

Reconstructing step by step never deactivates a point, and accumulates nothing: a step gives positions and no more. (The strain rate at each position can be found separately, as in Find plates and strain rates at static points.)

Data files

Both scripts read the same files:

topologies.gpml

Topological plate polygon and deforming network features. A point moves only while it is inside one of them.

rotations.rot

A rotation file. It must contain rotations for the plate IDs of the topological features, and of the features that the boundaries of the deforming networks are built from.

Files of these kinds are in the GPlates sample data.

Reconstruct points one time step at a time

Sample code

import pygplates


# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

# The points that we will reconstruct through the topological model.
# Each is the (latitude, longitude) of a point at the initial time.
initial_points = [(0, 155), (15, 160), (20, 155), (5, 170)]

# Our reconstruction will span from 30Ma to present day in 1 Myr steps.
initial_time = 30
time_increment = 1

# The positions of the points at the current time. They start at the initial positions.
points = initial_points

# 'time' = 30, 29, 28, ... , 1
for time in range(initial_time, 0, -time_increment):
    # The time that this step reconstructs the points to.
    # The last step stops at present day, even if 'time_increment' does not divide 'initial_time'.
    next_time = max(time - time_increment, 0)

    # Get a snapshot of our resolved topologies at the current 'time'.
    topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

    # Reconstruct the points from their positions at 'time' to their positions at 'next_time'.
    # Each point is moved by the resolved plate (or deforming network) that contains it at 'time'.
    # A point outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) is returned where it is.
    points = topological_snapshot.reconstruct_points(
            points,
            next_time,
            return_input_if_not_intersect=True)

    # Print the positions of the points at 'next_time'.
    print(f'Time {next_time}Ma:')
    for point in points:
        latitude, longitude = point.to_lat_lon()
        print(f'  lat: {latitude:f}, lon: {longitude:f}')

Details

A topological model is created from the topological features and the rotation file.

The topological features are not resolved here. That happens at each time, in the loop below.

# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

The points are given as (latitude, longitude) tuples. Each is where a point is at the initial time, not at present day.

Any of the forms of point that pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.reconstruct_points() accepts would do, such as a list of pygplates.PointOnSphere or a pygplates.MultiPointOnSphere.

# The points that we will reconstruct through the topological model.
# Each is the (latitude, longitude) of a point at the initial time.
initial_points = [(0, 155), (15, 160), (20, 155), (5, 170)]

The points are reconstructed from 30Ma to present day in steps of 1My.

Each step moves the points using the plates and networks as they are at the start of the step. Plate boundaries and plate motions change over time, so a small time step, such as 1My, is best.

# Our reconstruction will span from 30Ma to present day in 1 Myr steps.
initial_time = 30
time_increment = 1

points holds the positions of the points at the current time. It is replaced at every time step.

# The positions of the points at the current time. They start at the initial positions.
points = initial_points

Each pass of the loop is one time step, from time to next_time. The last one ends at present day, and is shorter than the others if time_increment does not divide initial_time.

# 'time' = 30, 29, 28, ... , 1
for time in range(initial_time, 0, -time_increment):
    # The time that this step reconstructs the points to.
    # The last step stops at present day, even if 'time_increment' does not divide 'initial_time'.
    next_time = max(time - time_increment, 0)

The topological features are resolved to the current time using pygplates.TopologicalModel.topological_snapshot().

# Get a snapshot of our resolved topologies at the current 'time'.
topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.reconstruct_points() reconstructs the points from the time of the snapshot to the time it is given. It returns a list with a pygplates.PointOnSphere for each point, in the order of the points.

Here next_time is younger than the time of the snapshot, so the points move forward in time. A time that is older moves them backward in time.

Where a deforming network overlays a rigid plate, a point inside both is reconstructed by the network.

A point in the deforming region of a network is moved by interpolating the motions of the vertices of the network’s triangulation around it. Natural neighbour interpolation is used unless the use_natural_neighbour_interpolation argument is False, and then barycentric interpolation.

A point that is outside all the resolved plates and networks cannot be reconstructed. By default its entry in the list is None. With return_input_if_not_intersect set to True, as here, the point is returned where it is, so every entry is a point and the list can be reconstructed again at the next time step. (A list with None in it cannot: the None entries have to be removed first.)

# Reconstruct the points from their positions at 'time' to their positions at 'next_time'.
# Each point is moved by the resolved plate (or deforming network) that contains it at 'time'.
# A point outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks) is returned where it is.
points = topological_snapshot.reconstruct_points(
        points,
        next_time,
        return_input_if_not_intersect=True)

Finally the positions of the points at the end of the time step are printed.

# Print the positions of the points at 'next_time'.
print(f'Time {next_time}Ma:')
for point in points:
    latitude, longitude = point.to_lat_lon()
    print(f'  lat: {latitude:f}, lon: {longitude:f}')

Note

These are the positions that pygplates.TopologicalModel.reconstruct_geometry() gives at the same times, for the same initial points and time step, when its deactivate_points argument is None. By default it deactivates points, which this script does not: a point on a subducting plate is carried to the trench and across it, and from then on it moves with the overriding plate (or with a deforming network there).

Reconstruct each point using the plate or network that contains it

This example reconstructs the same points to the same positions, but one point at a time. At each time step it finds the plate or network that contains each point, and reconstructs the point using that. It prints a line whenever a point is in a different plate or network than it was a time step before.

Sample code

import pygplates


# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

# The points that we will reconstruct through the topological model.
# Each is the (latitude, longitude) of a point at the initial time.
points = [(0, 155), (15, 160), (20, 155), (5, 170)]

# Our reconstruction will span from 30Ma to present day in 1 Myr steps.
initial_time = 30
time_increment = 1

# The plate (or deforming network) that contained each point at the previous time step, such as 'plate 901',
# 'network 608' or 'no plate or network'. None means that there has been no time step yet.
previous_topologies = [None] * len(points)

# 'time' = 30, 29, 28, ... , 1
for time in range(initial_time, 0, -time_increment):
    # The time that this step reconstructs the points to.
    # The last step stops at present day, even if 'time_increment' does not divide 'initial_time'.
    next_time = max(time - time_increment, 0)

    # Get a snapshot of our resolved topologies at the current 'time'.
    topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

    # Find the resolved plate (or deforming network) that contains each point at the current 'time'.
    point_locations = topological_snapshot.get_point_locations(points)

    reconstructed_points = []
    topologies = []

    for point_index, point in enumerate(points):
        point_location = point_locations[point_index]

        # The resolved plate that contains the point or, if the point is in a deforming network, the resolved network.
        resolved_topology = (point_location.located_in_resolved_boundary() or
                             point_location.located_in_resolved_network())

        if resolved_topology:
            # Reconstruct the point from its position at 'time' to its position at 'next_time'.
            # A rigid plate rotates the point, and a deforming network deforms it.
            reconstructed_point = resolved_topology.reconstruct_point(point, next_time)
            # Label the plate (or deforming network) by its plate ID, and by whether it is a plate or a network,
            # since a network can have the plate ID of the plate it overlays.
            plate_id = resolved_topology.get_feature().get_reconstruction_plate_id()
            if isinstance(resolved_topology, pygplates.ResolvedTopologicalNetwork):
                topology = f'network {plate_id}'
            else:
                topology = f'plate {plate_id}'
        else:
            # The point is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks), so leave it where it is.
            reconstructed_point = pygplates.PointOnSphere(point)
            topology = 'no plate or network'

        # Report a point that is in a different plate (or deforming network) than it was at the previous time step.
        previous_topology = previous_topologies[point_index]
        if time != initial_time and topology != previous_topology:
            print(f'Point {point_index} was in {previous_topology} at {time + time_increment}Ma '
                  f'and is in {topology} at {time}Ma')

        reconstructed_points.append(reconstructed_point)
        topologies.append(topology)

    # The reconstructed points are the points of the next time step.
    points = reconstructed_points
    previous_topologies = topologies

# Print the positions of the points at present day.
for point_index, point in enumerate(points):
    latitude, longitude = point.to_lat_lon()
    print(f'Point {point_index} is at lat: {latitude:f}, lon: {longitude:f} at present day')

Details

The topological model, points and time steps are the same as in the first example.

# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

# The points that we will reconstruct through the topological model.
# Each is the (latitude, longitude) of a point at the initial time.
points = [(0, 155), (15, 160), (20, 155), (5, 170)]

# Our reconstruction will span from 30Ma to present day in 1 Myr steps.
initial_time = 30
time_increment = 1

The plate or network that contains each point is kept from one time step to the next, so that a change can be noticed.

# The plate (or deforming network) that contained each point at the previous time step, such as 'plate 901',
# 'network 608' or 'no plate or network'. None means that there has been no time step yet.
previous_topologies = [None] * len(points)

At each time, pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.get_point_locations() returns the location of each point in the resolved topologies, in the order of the points.

# Find the resolved plate (or deforming network) that contains each point at the current 'time'.
point_locations = topological_snapshot.get_point_locations(points)

Each point is visited with its location.

for point_index, point in enumerate(points):
    point_location = point_locations[point_index]

pygplates.TopologyPointLocation.located_in_resolved_boundary() returns the resolved topological boundary (the rigid plate) that contains the point, or None.

pygplates.TopologyPointLocation.located_in_resolved_network() returns the resolved topological network that contains the point, or None.

A point is located in one or the other, not both: where a network overlays a plate, the point is located in the network. A point located in neither is outside all the plates and networks.

# The resolved plate that contains the point or, if the point is in a deforming network, the resolved network.
resolved_topology = (point_location.located_in_resolved_boundary() or
                     point_location.located_in_resolved_network())

Both classes have a reconstruct_point() method that reconstructs one point from the time the topology was resolved at to another time (see pygplates.ResolvedTopologicalBoundary.reconstruct_point() and pygplates.ResolvedTopologicalNetwork.reconstruct_point()).

A plate rotates the point by the motion of its plate ID. A network deforms a point that is in its deforming region, and rotates a point that is in one of its interior rigid blocks with that block.

The plate ID of the plate (or of the network) is read from its feature. It is not what moves a point in a network: that comes from the features the network is built from.

The plate or network is labelled by its plate ID and by whether it is a plate or a network, such as plate 901 or network 608. A deforming network can have the plate ID of the plate it overlays, so the plate ID alone would not tell the two apart.

A point outside all the plates and networks is left where it is, as it was in the first example, and labelled no plate or network.

if resolved_topology:
    # Reconstruct the point from its position at 'time' to its position at 'next_time'.
    # A rigid plate rotates the point, and a deforming network deforms it.
    reconstructed_point = resolved_topology.reconstruct_point(point, next_time)
    # Label the plate (or deforming network) by its plate ID, and by whether it is a plate or a network,
    # since a network can have the plate ID of the plate it overlays.
    plate_id = resolved_topology.get_feature().get_reconstruction_plate_id()
    if isinstance(resolved_topology, pygplates.ResolvedTopologicalNetwork):
        topology = f'network {plate_id}'
    else:
        topology = f'plate {plate_id}'
else:
    # The point is outside all the resolved plates (and deforming networks), so leave it where it is.
    reconstructed_point = pygplates.PointOnSphere(point)
    topology = 'no plate or network'

A point whose plate or network differs from the one a time step before has passed from one to another, or into or out of the plates altogether.

The labels are compared, rather than the resolved plates, because each snapshot has resolved topologies of its own: the plate that contains a point at one time is never the same object as the plate that contains it at another.

Nor are the feature IDs of the plates compared. A model can define a plate by a succession of features, each for its own span of time (the GPlates sample data does), and a point would seem to change plates whenever its plate changed from one feature to the next. The labels do not tell apart two networks that have the same plate ID, though.

This is where a script can apply a rule of its own. For example, it could stop following a point that passes from a subducting plate to the plate overriding it.

# Report a point that is in a different plate (or deforming network) than it was at the previous time step.
previous_topology = previous_topologies[point_index]
if time != initial_time and topology != previous_topology:
    print(f'Point {point_index} was in {previous_topology} at {time + time_increment}Ma '
          f'and is in {topology} at {time}Ma')

The reconstructed points, and the labels of the plates and networks they were in, are the starting points of the next time step.

# The reconstructed points are the points of the next time step.
points = reconstructed_points
previous_topologies = topologies

After the last time step the points are at their present day positions.

# Print the positions of the points at present day.
for point_index, point in enumerate(points):
    latitude, longitude = point.to_lat_lon()
    print(f'Point {point_index} is at lat: {latitude:f}, lon: {longitude:f} at present day')

Note

pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.reconstruct_points() gives the same positions in one call, and is noticeably faster, since getting the plate or network out of each point’s location takes time. Reconstruct each point by itself, as here, only when the plate or network of each point is needed.
