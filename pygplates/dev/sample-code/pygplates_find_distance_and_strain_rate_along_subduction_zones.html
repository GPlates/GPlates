Find the distance and strain rate along subduction zones

This example samples points along the subduction zones of resolved topological plates, and finds for each point:

  • how far along its subduction zone the point is, from the start of the subduction zone and from its end, and

  • the strain rate of the overriding plate, and of the subducting plate, at the point.

Both come from pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.calculate_plate_boundary_statistics(), which calculates a pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic at each of a set of points spaced uniformly along the plate boundaries.

The distances make a profile along each subduction zone: any other statistic of the points can be plotted against the distance along the trench, or against the distance to the nearer end of the trench.

The strain rate of a plate is only non-zero where that plate is a deforming network, such as a deforming zone along the overriding side of a trench.

See also

Two other examples use the same statistics. Find divergence at subduction zones and convergence at ridges uses the velocity at which the plates converge at each point, and Sample strain rates inside the overriding plate at subduction zones samples the strain rate a set distance inside the overriding plate, rather than at the trench itself.

Data files

topologies.gpml

Topological plate polygon features, with or without deforming network features, and the boundary section features that they are built from. Subduction zones are recognised by the feature type (pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_subduction_zone) of the boundary section features, and each needs a subduction polarity of 'Left' or 'Right' (others are skipped).

rotations.rot

A rotation file. It must contain rotations for the plate IDs of the topological features, and of the features that the boundaries of the deforming networks are built from.

Files of these kinds are in the GPlates sample data.

Sample code

import math
import pygplates


# Create a topological model from the topological plate polygon features (can also include deforming networks)
# and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

# Get a snapshot of our resolved topologies at 10Ma.
time = 10
topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

# Calculate statistics at points spaced 1 degree apart along the plate boundaries that are subduction zones.
plate_boundary_statistics = topological_snapshot.calculate_plate_boundary_statistics(
        math.radians(1),  # 1 degree spacing between points
        boundary_section_filter=pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_subduction_zone)

# Iterate over the uniformly spaced points. They are in order along each subduction zone.
for stat in plate_boundary_statistics:

    # The subduction zone feature that the point is on.
    subduction_zone_feature = stat.shared_sub_segment.get_feature()

    # The subduction polarity says which side of the subduction zone the overriding plate is on.
    subduction_polarity = subduction_zone_feature.get_enumeration(pygplates.PropertyName.gpml_subduction_polarity)
    if subduction_polarity == 'Left':
        overriding_plate_strain_rate = stat.left_plate_strain_rate
        subducting_plate_strain_rate = stat.right_plate_strain_rate
    elif subduction_polarity == 'Right':
        overriding_plate_strain_rate = stat.right_plate_strain_rate
        subducting_plate_strain_rate = stat.left_plate_strain_rate
    else:
        # Subduction polarity is not defined or known, so skip the point.
        continue

    # The distances (in kms) along the subduction zone from its start to the point, and from the point to its end.
    distance_from_start_in_kms = (stat.distance_from_start_of_topological_section *
                                  pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)
    distance_to_end_in_kms = (stat.distance_to_end_of_topological_section *
                              pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)

    # The same distances, but along only the part of the subduction zone that the plates at this point share.
    distance_from_start_of_shared_part_in_kms = (stat.distance_from_start_of_shared_sub_segment *
                                                 pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)
    distance_to_end_of_shared_part_in_kms = (stat.distance_to_end_of_shared_sub_segment *
                                             pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)

    # The distance (in kms) from the point to the nearer end of the subduction zone.
    distance_to_nearest_edge_in_kms = min(distance_from_start_in_kms, distance_to_end_in_kms)

    latitude, longitude = stat.boundary_point.to_lat_lon()
    print(f'"{subduction_zone_feature.get_name()}" at ({latitude:.2f}, {longitude:.2f}):')
    print(f'  {distance_from_start_in_kms:.0f} kms from its start and {distance_to_end_in_kms:.0f} kms from its end '
          f'(nearest end {distance_to_nearest_edge_in_kms:.0f} kms)')
    print(f'  {distance_from_start_of_shared_part_in_kms:.0f} kms from the start and '
          f'{distance_to_end_of_shared_part_in_kms:.0f} kms from the end of the part shared by the same plates')
    print(f'  dilatation rate of the overriding plate {overriding_plate_strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s '
          f'and of the subducting plate {subducting_plate_strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s')

Details

A topological model is created from the topological features and the rotation file.

# Create a topological model from the topological plate polygon features (can also include deforming networks)
# and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

The topological features are resolved at 10Ma using pygplates.TopologicalModel.topological_snapshot().

# Get a snapshot of our resolved topologies at 10Ma.
time = 10
topological_snapshot = topological_model.topological_snapshot(time)

pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.calculate_plate_boundary_statistics() generates points along the plate boundaries and returns a pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic for each.

Here the points are 1 degree apart, and the boundary_section_filter argument restricts them to the boundary sections whose feature type is pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_subduction_zone.

# Calculate statistics at points spaced 1 degree apart along the plate boundaries that are subduction zones.
plate_boundary_statistics = topological_snapshot.calculate_plate_boundary_statistics(
        math.radians(1),  # 1 degree spacing between points
        boundary_section_filter=pygplates.FeatureType.gpml_subduction_zone)

The statistics are returned one subduction zone after another, and those of each subduction zone are in order along it, from its start to its end.

# Iterate over the uniformly spaced points. They are in order along each subduction zone.
for stat in plate_boundary_statistics:

A subduction zone is one feature, but different plates can share different parts of it. For example, where the boundary between two subducting plates meets the trench, the subducting plate changes from one to the other.

Each part that the same plates share is a shared sub-segment, and pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.shared_sub_segment is the one that the point is on.

The subduction zone feature is its feature.

# The subduction zone feature that the point is on.
subduction_zone_feature = stat.shared_sub_segment.get_feature()

The strain rates of the plates to the left and to the right of the point are pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.left_plate_strain_rate and pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.right_plate_strain_rate. Left and right are as seen when following the points of the subduction zone in order.

The subduction polarity of the subduction zone is the side that its overriding plate is on, so it says which of the two is the overriding plate.

Each is a pygplates.StrainRate: the strain rate of that plate at the point itself, on the boundary of the plate.

It is zero for a rigid plate, and for a deforming network if the point is in one of the network’s interior rigid blocks. It is also zero where there is no plate on that side at all.

Where a deforming network overlays a rigid plate along that side of the boundary, the network is the plate on that side.

# The subduction polarity says which side of the subduction zone the overriding plate is on.
subduction_polarity = subduction_zone_feature.get_enumeration(pygplates.PropertyName.gpml_subduction_polarity)
if subduction_polarity == 'Left':
    overriding_plate_strain_rate = stat.left_plate_strain_rate
    subducting_plate_strain_rate = stat.right_plate_strain_rate
elif subduction_polarity == 'Right':
    overriding_plate_strain_rate = stat.right_plate_strain_rate
    subducting_plate_strain_rate = stat.left_plate_strain_rate
else:
    # Subduction polarity is not defined or known, so skip the point.
    continue

pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.distance_from_start_of_topological_section is the distance from the start of the subduction zone to the point, and pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.distance_to_end_of_topological_section is the distance from the point to its end.

Both are measured along the subduction zone, following its bends. They are in radians, so they are multiplied by the radius of the Earth to get kilometres.

The subduction zone starts where the points of its geometry start, so the distances follow the order of those points. To be exact, the start and the end are those of the part of the geometry that is a plate boundary: a length of the line that sticks out past the plates at either end is not counted.

# The distances (in kms) along the subduction zone from its start to the point, and from the point to its end.
distance_from_start_in_kms = (stat.distance_from_start_of_topological_section *
                              pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)
distance_to_end_in_kms = (stat.distance_to_end_of_topological_section *
                          pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)

pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.distance_from_start_of_shared_sub_segment and pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.distance_to_end_of_shared_sub_segment are the same two distances, but to the ends of the shared sub-segment that the point is on. So they start again from zero wherever the plates on either side change. Where the same two plates share the whole subduction zone, they equal the distances above, or nearly. A shared sub-segment includes half of any rubber band (the line that joins the end of the subduction zone to the next section of a plate boundary where the two do not intersect), so on a real model these distances can be a few kilometres longer: 3 to 4 km at 8 of 603 points on the Zahirovic et al. (2022) model at 10Ma.

# The same distances, but along only the part of the subduction zone that the plates at this point share.
distance_from_start_of_shared_part_in_kms = (stat.distance_from_start_of_shared_sub_segment *
                                             pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)
distance_to_end_of_shared_part_in_kms = (stat.distance_to_end_of_shared_sub_segment *
                                         pygplates.Earth.mean_radius_in_kms)

The smaller of the two distances along the subduction zone is the distance to its nearer end.

# The distance (in kms) from the point to the nearer end of the subduction zone.
distance_to_nearest_edge_in_kms = min(distance_from_start_in_kms, distance_to_end_in_kms)

Finally the point is printed with its distances and the dilatation rate of the plate on each side.

The dilatation rate is the rate of change of crustal area per unit area (in units of 1/second). It is positive where the crust is extending and negative where it is contracting.

latitude, longitude = stat.boundary_point.to_lat_lon()
print(f'"{subduction_zone_feature.get_name()}" at ({latitude:.2f}, {longitude:.2f}):')
print(f'  {distance_from_start_in_kms:.0f} kms from its start and {distance_to_end_in_kms:.0f} kms from its end '
      f'(nearest end {distance_to_nearest_edge_in_kms:.0f} kms)')
print(f'  {distance_from_start_of_shared_part_in_kms:.0f} kms from the start and '
      f'{distance_to_end_of_shared_part_in_kms:.0f} kms from the end of the part shared by the same plates')
print(f'  dilatation rate of the overriding plate {overriding_plate_strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s '
      f'and of the subducting plate {subducting_plate_strain_rate.get_dilatation_rate():.3e} 1/s')

Note

The plates to the left and right of a point, which the two strain rates belong to, are pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.left_plate and pygplates.PlateBoundaryStatistic.right_plate. Each is a pygplates.TopologyPointLocation, which says whether there is a rigid plate, a deforming network or nothing on that side (as in Sample strain rates inside the overriding plate at subduction zones).

Note

By default no points are generated along the parts of a deforming network’s boundary that are not also plate boundaries, such as the edge of a deforming zone that crosses the interior of a plate. Set the include_network_boundaries argument to True to generate them. One of the two strain rates is then that of the network. The other is zero where the network overlays a rigid plate there, since that plate is what is on the other side.
