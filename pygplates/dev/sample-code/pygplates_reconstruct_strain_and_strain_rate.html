Reconstruct strain and strain rate

This example reconstructs and deforms points through topological plate polygons and deforming networks, from a past geological time to present day. At each time along the way it finds:

  • the reconstructed positions of the initial points,

  • their accumulated strains (since the initial time), and

  • their instantaneous strain rates and velocities.

This is the equivalent of the deformation export in GPlates.

Data files

topologies.gpml

Topological plate polygon and deforming network features. There must be networks at 30Ma, since the initial points are chosen from them. Leave out inactive networks, such as those in Zahirovic_etal_2022_Deforming_Networks_Inactive.gpml in the GPlates sample data, so that the points are chosen from networks that are deforming. Strain only accumulates, and the strain rate is only non-zero, while a point is inside a deforming network.

rotations.rot

A rotation file. It must contain rotations for the plate IDs of the topological features.

Files of these kinds are in the GPlates sample data.

Sample code

import sys
import pygplates


# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

# Our reconstruction will span from 30Ma to present day in 1 Myr intervals.
initial_time = 30
# The time increment must divide 'initial_time' exactly ('reconstruct_geometry()' raises ValueError otherwise).
time_increment = 1

# The initial points that we will reconstruct (and deform) through the topological model.
# They are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart, from latitude -60 to 60
# (south to north): the first four that are in the deforming region of a network at the initial time.
grid_points = [(latitude, longitude) for latitude in range(-60, 70, 10) for longitude in range(-180, 180, 10)]
grid_point_locations = topological_model.topological_snapshot(initial_time).get_point_locations(grid_points)
initial_points = [point for point, point_location in zip(grid_points, grid_point_locations)
                  if point_location.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region()][:4]

# Stop if there are none: there are no deforming networks at the initial time, or they are too small for the grid.
if not initial_points:
    sys.exit(f'No grid point is in the deforming region of a network at {initial_time}Ma')

# Reconstruct the initial points through the topological model from initial time to present day.
reconstructed_time_span = topological_model.reconstruct_geometry(
        initial_points,
        initial_time,
        time_increment=time_increment)

# Get the history of reconstructed positions, strains, strain rates and velocities of the initial points from initial time to present day.
for time in range(initial_time, -1, -time_increment):  # initial_time, initial_time - time_increment, ..., 0
    # Reconstructed positions at the current 'time'.
    reconstructed_points = reconstructed_time_span.get_geometry_points(time)

    # If every point has been deactivated by the current 'time' then there are no positions (or anything else) to get.
    if reconstructed_points is None:
        continue

    # The resolved topologies (rigid plates and deforming networks) in which the reconstructed positions are located at the current 'time'.
    reconstructed_topology_point_locations = reconstructed_time_span.get_topology_point_locations(time)

    # The velocity for each point at the current 'time' in cms/yr calculated with a 1 Myr time interval from 'time+1' to 'time'.
    reconstructed_velocities = reconstructed_time_span.get_velocities(
            time,
            velocity_delta_time=1.0,
            velocity_delta_time_type=pygplates.VelocityDeltaTimeType.t_plus_delta_t_to_t,
            velocity_units=pygplates.VelocityUnits.cms_per_yr)

    # The accumulated strain and instantaneous strain rate for each point at the current 'time'.
    reconstructed_strains = reconstructed_time_span.get_strains(time)
    reconstructed_strain_rates = reconstructed_time_span.get_strain_rates(time)

    # For each point extract various quantities from its strain and strain rate.
    num_reconstructed_points = len(reconstructed_points)
    for reconstructed_point_index in range(num_reconstructed_points):

        # Dilatation measures the change in crustal area with respect to the initial area around the current point.
        dilatation = reconstructed_strains[reconstructed_point_index].get_dilatation()
        # Principal-strain is the maximum and minimum strains (along principal axes), and the angle (radians) of the major principal axis (clockwise from North).
        max_strain, min_strain, major_azimuth_radians = reconstructed_strains[reconstructed_point_index].get_principal_strain(
                principal_angle_type=pygplates.PrincipalAngleType.major_azimuth)

        # Dilatation-rate measures the rate of change of crustal area per unit area (in units of 1/second) around the current point.
        dilatation_rate = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_dilatation_rate()
        # Total-strain-rate measures the strain-rate magnitude (in units of 1/second), including both the normal (extension/compression) and shear components.
        total_strain_rate = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_total_strain_rate()
        # Strain-rate-style is a measure categorising the type of deformation.
        # A value of -1 represents contraction (eg, pure reverse faulting), 0 represents pure strike-slip faulting and 1 represents extension (eg, pure normal faulting).
        strain_rate_style = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_strain_rate_style()

        # Print the dilatation, dilatation rate, total strain rate and strain rate style of the current point.
        print(f'Time {time}Ma, point {reconstructed_point_index}: dilatation {dilatation:.3f}, '
              f'dilatation rate {dilatation_rate:.3e} 1/s, total strain rate {total_strain_rate:.3e} 1/s, '
              f'strain rate style {strain_rate_style:.2f}')

Details

A topological model is created from the topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and the rotation file.

Here there is one file of topological features and one rotation file. More than one of each can be given, and a pygplates.RotationModel can be given in place of the rotation files.

# Create a topological model from our topological features (plate polygons and deforming networks) and rotation file(s).
topological_model = pygplates.TopologicalModel('topologies.gpml', 'rotations.rot')

The initial points are chosen from the networks, rather than written into the script, so that the example works with any topological features that have deforming networks at the initial time, 30Ma.

They are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart, from latitude -60 to 60. It stops at 60 degrees because, in the GPlates sample data, the grid points nearer the poles happen to fall where the networks barely deform at 30Ma, so they would make poor examples. The grid is scanned from south to north, and the first points found are used. In the GPlates sample data, three of the first four points found from north to south barely deform.

pygplates.TopologicalModel.topological_snapshot() resolves the topologies at the initial time, and pygplates.TopologicalSnapshot.get_point_locations() returns the location of each grid point in them. The first four points that pygplates.TopologyPointLocation.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region() places in the deforming region of a network are kept. If there are none, the script stops with an error message that says so.

# The initial points that we will reconstruct (and deform) through the topological model.
# They are chosen from a coarse grid of (latitude, longitude) points, 10 degrees apart, from latitude -60 to 60
# (south to north): the first four that are in the deforming region of a network at the initial time.
grid_points = [(latitude, longitude) for latitude in range(-60, 70, 10) for longitude in range(-180, 180, 10)]
grid_point_locations = topological_model.topological_snapshot(initial_time).get_point_locations(grid_points)
initial_points = [point for point, point_location in zip(grid_points, grid_point_locations)
                  if point_location.located_in_resolved_network_deforming_region()][:4]

# Stop if there are none: there are no deforming networks at the initial time, or they are too small for the grid.
if not initial_points:
    sys.exit(f'No grid point is in the deforming region of a network at {initial_time}Ma')

pygplates.TopologicalModel.reconstruct_geometry() reconstructs (and deforms) the initial_points from their positions at initial_time to present day, in 1 Myr intervals.

It returns a reconstructed geometry time span. This holds the history of the reconstructed point positions, and of the strains and strain rates at the points.

The strain and strain rate of a point change while it is passing through a deforming network. A point in a rigid plate (outside all deforming networks) has a strain rate of zero, so it accumulates no strain there.

The deactivate_points argument is not given. So points are deactivated in the default way, which is the way that GPlates does it (with Reconstruct using topologies in a green layer). This means an initial point on oceanic crust can be subducted and disappear (be deactivated).

# Reconstruct the initial points through the topological model from initial time to present day.
reconstructed_time_span = topological_model.reconstruct_geometry(
        initial_points,
        initial_time,
        time_increment=time_increment)

pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_geometry_points() returns the reconstructed positions at the current time. Some of the initial points may have been deactivated by then, so reconstructed_points can have fewer points than initial_points.

# Reconstructed positions at the current 'time'.
reconstructed_points = reconstructed_time_span.get_geometry_points(time)

If all the points have been deactivated by the current time then pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_geometry_points() returns None rather than an empty list, and so do the other queries below. So the script tests for that first, and skips the current time.

# If every point has been deactivated by the current 'time' then there are no positions (or anything else) to get.
if reconstructed_points is None:
    continue

Each of the queries below returns a list with one value for each point in reconstructed_points, in the same order.

pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_topology_point_locations() returns the resolved topology (rigid plate or deforming network) that each reconstructed position is in at the current time.

# The resolved topologies (rigid plates and deforming networks) in which the reconstructed positions are located at the current 'time'.
reconstructed_topology_point_locations = reconstructed_time_span.get_topology_point_locations(time)

pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_velocities() returns the velocity of each point. Here the velocities are in cms/yr, and are calculated over a 1 Myr interval from time+1 to time.

# The velocity for each point at the current 'time' in cms/yr calculated with a 1 Myr time interval from 'time+1' to 'time'.
reconstructed_velocities = reconstructed_time_span.get_velocities(
        time,
        velocity_delta_time=1.0,
        velocity_delta_time_type=pygplates.VelocityDeltaTimeType.t_plus_delta_t_to_t,
        velocity_units=pygplates.VelocityUnits.cms_per_yr)

pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_strains() returns the accumulated strain of each point at the current time, and pygplates.ReconstructedGeometryTimeSpan.get_strain_rates() returns its instantaneous strain rate.

# The accumulated strain and instantaneous strain rate for each point at the current 'time'.
reconstructed_strains = reconstructed_time_span.get_strains(time)
reconstructed_strain_rates = reconstructed_time_span.get_strain_rates(time)

For each point, the script then gets several quantities from its strain and its strain rate. pygplates.Strain and pygplates.StrainRate define these quantities.

The dilatation is the change in crustal area at the point, as a proportion of the initial area. It is positive where the crust has expanded and negative where it has contracted.

# Dilatation measures the change in crustal area with respect to the initial area around the current point.
dilatation = reconstructed_strains[reconstructed_point_index].get_dilatation()

The principal strains are the maximum and minimum strains, which are along the major and minor principal axes. Each is the change in length per unit initial length (since the initial time) in the direction of its axis.

The angle (in radians) is the direction of the major principal axis. With pygplates.PrincipalAngleType.major_azimuth it is measured clockwise from North, in the range \([0,2\pi]\).

# Principal-strain is the maximum and minimum strains (along principal axes), and the angle (radians) of the major principal axis (clockwise from North).
max_strain, min_strain, major_azimuth_radians = reconstructed_strains[reconstructed_point_index].get_principal_strain(
        principal_angle_type=pygplates.PrincipalAngleType.major_azimuth)

The dilatation rate is the rate of change of crustal area per unit area at the point, in units of 1/second. It is positive where the crust is expanding and negative where it is contracting.

# Dilatation-rate measures the rate of change of crustal area per unit area (in units of 1/second) around the current point.
dilatation_rate = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_dilatation_rate()

The total strain rate is the magnitude of the strain rate, in units of 1/second. It includes both the normal (extension and compression) and the shear components.

# Total-strain-rate measures the strain-rate magnitude (in units of 1/second), including both the normal (extension/compression) and shear components.
total_strain_rate = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_total_strain_rate()

The strain rate style categorises the type of deformation. A value of -1 represents contraction (eg, pure reverse faulting), 0 represents pure strike-slip faulting and 1 represents extension (eg, pure normal faulting). It is NaN where the strain rate is zero, as it is for every point on a rigid plate.

# Strain-rate-style is a measure categorising the type of deformation.
# A value of -1 represents contraction (eg, pure reverse faulting), 0 represents pure strike-slip faulting and 1 represents extension (eg, pure normal faulting).
strain_rate_style = reconstructed_strain_rates[reconstructed_point_index].get_strain_rate_style()
